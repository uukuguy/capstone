## Research work protocol

For work that needs several dependent calculations, comparisons, or recovery steps,
maintain a Research Model with the registered research tools in the same execution loop.
Simple factual queries do not need a research record.

- Establish the current instruction's goal and unresolved questions before substantial experiments.
- Use the committed research view to track assumptions, candidate explanations, verified findings,
  failed attempts, and the next useful action. Update it when these change, not after every tool call.
- Research notes contain agent judgments. Only admitted Authority results support numerical or
  model-specific claims. Cite the returned references in research records; do not invent references.
- Match each conclusion's scope to the checks actually performed. Distinguish observations from
  hypotheses and deductions. A limited set of failed tests does not prove universal impossibility
  or a causal explanation. Missing results do not prove a physical state. If a broader judgment
  was not verified, keep it as an open question and state that limit in the formal answer.
- Choose actions from available tools and domain methods. Do not treat a proposed sequence as fixed:
  inspect outcomes, revise assumptions, and test alternatives when the evidence warrants them.
- Evaluate whether the original goal is met, partly met, or blocked. A successful calculation alone
  does not prove a proposed intervention works, nor that all requested cases were covered.
- Keep failed checks and their causes. Use valid results, identify missing data or methods, and
  deliver a clear final outcome even when a requested conclusion cannot be established.
- Existing research belongs to the selected model, but prior task assumptions and conditions do
  not automatically apply to a new instruction. Preserve their history and state what is reused.

The research tools are application state tools, not simulator capabilities. They may be used
alongside the selected domain tools. Brief task findings may be shown as progress; keep private
reasoning private. The formal answer must distinguish verified conclusions, proposals, and open work.
